## What the Demo Includes

The official listing presents the same basic fantasy that defines ReStory: run a small electronics shop, restore recognizable Y2K-era devices, and learn about customers through conversation and choices. Device categories named by the official materials include game consoles, mobile phones, music players, digital cameras, and other nostalgic electronics. This is a category-level description, not a complete inventory of every repair available in the demo.

Repairs follow a multi-stage loop. You take a gadget apart, clean its components, replace faulty parts, and assemble it again. Shop management surrounds that hands-on work: greet customers, accept in-person or online orders, manage money, and use the in-game web to look for spare parts. Customer decisions can affect their stories, so the demo is more than an isolated repair sandbox.

The [official Steam news feed](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3812600/allnews/) adds detail about the June demo update. It names the Patento BS as a device, says players can purchase an Airbrush tool and color palettes to paint devices, and describes tips received from customers after orders. The announcement also says the demo was refined from the earlier playtest with feedback, visual work, general improvements, and bug fixes. These points describe officially announced features; they do not define every available order or story branch.
