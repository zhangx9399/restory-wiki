## What to Know Before Starting

ReStory combines device repair, shop management, and customer stories. The official Steam store confirms greeting customers, accepting devices, finances, an in-game spare-parts web, disassembly, cleaning, component replacement, reassembly, and in-person and online orders. These systems do not define a mandatory opening script.

Treat this route as a learning framework. Tutorial wording, jobs, tools, and interface placement can vary by build or progression. Read the current objective and trust visible prompts and test feedback. Choices can affect the branching story, so read dialogue carefully.

## Your First Repair Route

Begin by reading the customer's request and inspecting the device before you remove anything. Identify the requested symptom or visible condition, then learn how the current interface selects a part and tool. A careful first pass gives you a mental picture of where covers, screws, and internal pieces belong. It also reduces the chance of replacing something that only needed cleaning.

A useful route is inspect, disassemble, clean or replace as required, reassemble, test, and return. This mirrors the official description but does not guarantee every job exposes those stages in order. Tutorial gates and version-specific objectives may use only part of the loop; follow the brief when it differs.

After each meaningful change, confirm the objective advanced, the part moved correctly, or the device test responded. These checkpoints locate mistakes without inventing a universal reset method.

## Cleaning and Reassembly Basics

Official material confirms an in-depth cleaning and restoration system, but it does not publish one permanent cleaning button or station path. Remove the component the current job identifies, engage the cleaning interaction shown in your build, and watch for visual progress. A part merely resting near a work area may not be selected. If dirt does not change, recheck the chosen component, interaction state, and visible prompt before assuming a bug.

During disassembly, notice orientation and nesting, working from exterior pieces toward the requested area. Reassemble from your observations and placement feedback. If a piece will not seat, check for a missing lower layer, wrong orientation, or unfinished objective. Never force a community sequence onto another device or patch.

## Painting and Shop Customization

Gadget painting and shop customization are separate systems. Official Steam news documents an Airbrush and gadget palettes. The <a href="https://store.steampowered.com/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/1839676055897780" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">official ReStory launch announcement</a> documents paintable walls, changeable shelf styles, storage shelving for licenses, and placeable decorations. Neither source says beginners must learn both immediately or that one tool controls both targets.

Focus first on the active repair. For painting, follow the visible request and current interface rather than assuming a permanent key, unlock level, or scoring formula. Treat shop layout separately. Postponing cosmetic experiments until the repair loop feels familiar is advice, not a hidden progression rule.

## Managing Time, Parts, and Customer Work

The official store confirms finances, web searches for spare parts, and in-person and online orders. It does not publish a universal schedule, guaranteed profit formula, or perfect priority. Buy a component only when the job, inspection, or testing supports it.

For a calm workflow, keep one short mental queue: the current customer's request, the device's present state, and the next verified action. Finish or park a job at a clear checkpoint before switching attention. Read dialogue carefully because customer choices can affect the branching story. If several orders are available, use their visible conditions and your current resources; a player strategy from another save is not an official optimization rule.

## Beginner Mistakes to Avoid

- Do not remove every component automatically. Expose what the current repair needs and observe how pieces fit.
- Do not treat cleaning audio, movement, or proximity alone as proof of completion. Look for visual progress and objective feedback.
- Do not replace a part before the job, inspection, or test supports that decision. Spare parts affect finances.
- Do not confuse gadget painting with shop customization. They change different targets.
- Do not copy fixed keys, unlock levels, or profit formulas from an undated post. Check the version and current prompts.
- Do not rush dialogue. Official material says choices can affect customer lives and the branching ending.

These habits reduce risk; they are not a scoring formula. Follow contrary tutorial instructions for that build and record the version when reporting problems.
