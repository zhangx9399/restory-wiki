## What to Know Before Starting

ReStory combines detailed device repair with shop management and customer stories. The official Steam store confirms the broad loop: greet customers, accept devices, manage finances, find spare parts through the in-game web, disassemble gadgets, clean them, replace faulty components, and assemble them again. It also confirms in-person and online orders. Those facts describe the game's systems, not a mandatory opening script.

Treat the route below as a learning framework. Tutorial wording, available jobs, tool access, and interface placement can change with the build or progression state. Read the current objective before acting, and use visible highlights, prompts, and test feedback as your local authority. Choices can affect the branching story, so dialogue deserves the same attention as screws and parts.

## Your First Repair Route

Begin by reading the customer's request and inspecting the device before you remove anything. Identify the requested symptom or visible condition, then learn how the current interface selects a part and tool. A careful first pass gives you a mental picture of where covers, screws, and internal pieces belong. It also reduces the chance of replacing something that only needed cleaning.

A useful route is inspect, disassemble, clean or replace as required, reassemble, test, and return. This mirrors the official repair description, but it is a conceptual route rather than a guarantee that every job exposes those stages in that order. Some customer work, tutorial gates, or version-specific objectives may emphasize only part of the loop. Follow the brief whenever it differs from this summary.

Pause after each meaningful change. Confirm that the objective advanced, the part moved to the intended state, or the device test reports a useful result. Small checkpoints make it easier to find the last incorrect action without inventing a universal reset method.

## Cleaning and Reassembly Basics

Official material confirms an in-depth cleaning and restoration system, but it does not publish one permanent cleaning button or station path. Remove the component the current job identifies, engage the cleaning interaction shown in your build, and watch for visual progress. A part merely resting near a work area may not be selected. If dirt does not change, recheck the chosen component, interaction state, and visible prompt before assuming a bug.

During disassembly, notice orientation and nesting. Work from exterior pieces toward the requested internal area, and avoid scattering unrelated parts. During reassembly, reverse your observed structure and use the game's placement feedback. If a piece will not seat, stop and inspect for a missing lower layer, wrong orientation, or unfinished objective. Do not force an exact community sequence onto a different device or patch.

## Painting and Shop Customization

Painting a customer's gadget and customizing the shop are separate creative systems. Official Steam news documents an Airbrush and color palettes for gadgets. The <a href="https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/3812600/view/1839676055897780" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">official ReStory launch announcement</a> separately documents paintable walls, changeable shelf styles, storage shelving for licenses, and placeable decorations. Neither source establishes that a beginner must learn both immediately or that the same tool controls both targets.

Keep early attention on the active repair. When painting becomes relevant, read the order's visible design request and use the current interface rather than assuming a permanent key, unlock level, or scoring formula. When shop customization appears, treat layout and decoration as a separate decision. It is reasonable to postpone cosmetic experimentation until the basic repair loop feels familiar; that is practical advice, not a hidden progression rule.

## Managing Time, Parts, and Customer Work

The official store says players manage finances, search the in-game web for spare parts, and fulfill both in-person and online orders. It does not publish a universal best schedule, guaranteed profit formula, or perfect order priority. Before buying a component, compare the job's stated need with what inspection and testing show. Avoid spending merely because a part is available.

For a calm workflow, keep one short mental queue: the current customer's request, the device's present state, and the next verified action. Finish or park a job at a clear checkpoint before switching attention. Read dialogue carefully because customer choices can affect the branching story. If several orders are available, use their visible conditions and your current resources; a player strategy from another save is not an official optimization rule.

## Beginner Mistakes to Avoid

- Do not remove every component automatically. Expose what the current repair needs and observe how pieces fit.
- Do not treat cleaning audio, movement, or proximity alone as proof of completion. Look for visual progress and objective feedback.
- Do not replace a part before the job, inspection, or test supports that decision. Spare parts affect finances.
- Do not confuse gadget painting with shop customization. They change different targets.
- Do not copy fixed keys, unlock levels, or profit formulas from an undated post. Check the version and current prompts.
- Do not rush dialogue. Official material says choices can affect customer lives and the branching ending.

These are risk-reduction habits, not a score-maximizing formula. If a current tutorial explicitly instructs something different, follow the tutorial for that build and record the version when reporting a problem.
