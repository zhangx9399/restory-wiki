## Shop Customization vs Gadget Painting

Shop customization changes the working environment around a repair. It covers the room's walls, its shelves, useful storage shelving, and decorative objects. Those choices affect how the store looks and how its visible surfaces and display space are presented. They do not repaint a customer's console, phone, camera, or music player. Keeping that boundary clear prevents two official announcements from being blended into one unsupported system.

Gadget painting acts on a device instead. The official Steam news describes purchasing an airbrush and color palettes to create designs on gadgets. That is evidence for device-level painting, not evidence that the same tool paints the shop walls. Conversely, the announcement about shop customization says the walls can be painted and shelves can be changed, but it does not say that shop finishes are applied through the gadget airbrush. Treat them as related creative features with different targets.

## Walls, Shelves, and Storage

Start a visual plan with the largest repeated elements. Walls occupy much of the visible room, so a restrained wall color can serve as the backdrop for devices and furniture. Shelf appearance then repeats across the shop and can either continue that palette or introduce contrast. The official news supports the ability to paint the walls and change the shelves; it does not enumerate every color, texture, or named shelf variant. A guide should therefore discuss roles and combinations without inventing a catalog.

Storage shelving deserves separate attention from ordinary display style because the official announcement assigns it a function. Additional storage with shelving can hold licenses and avoid taking up crucial desk space. That statement supports the existence and intended use of the storage shelves. It does not establish capacity numbers, automatic sorting, placement grids, or whether a license must remain visible after it is stored. Those details should be read from the current build rather than inferred.

A simple planning method is to choose one dominant visual direction, one shelf treatment, and a small set of accents. Then reserve the useful storage area before filling open space with decorative pieces. This method reduces visual clutter and protects the desk as a working focal point, but it is optional design advice. It is not a required sequence and does not imply that the game scores tidy arrangements.
